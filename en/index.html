Down to the detail

For every day, for many years

So that it stays with you for a long time, we also paid attention to the things you don't see at first glance.

The spout

We chose a balanced spout, neither too narrow nor too wide. That gives you room for different patterns, from a heart to a rosetta. The tip is rounded, so the milk flows evenly and stops cleanly at the end.

The wall

The LUN is made from one millimetre of 304 stainless steel, the whole jug including the handle. Many jugs this size are thinner. The extra weight sits well in the hand and makes pouring steadier.

The warmth

Stainless steel conducts heat well, so while you steam you can feel on the body how warm the milk is, and with a little practice you quickly get a feel for it. When it gets too hot to touch, the milk is ready. The handle stays comfortable.

The scale

A scale in millilitres and ounces is laser engraved inside. That way you can use the same amount of milk every morning without guessing. Because it's in the steel, it lasts in the dishwasher too.

The handle

The handle is joined without a seam or visible weld points. It looks calmer, is easier to keep clean, and helps you guide the jug as you pour.

Good to know

Size: The LUN holds 350 ml. Milk gains volume when you steam it, so that's enough for one cappuccino or latte, two flat whites or one matcha latte.

Care: All four colours can go in the dishwasher. If you wash it by hand and dry it quickly, the finish stays free of water marks for longest. Please no abrasive cleaners or steel wool.

Packaging: The LUN comes in a cotton bag and a natural cardboard box, with no plastic at all. You can keep using the bag afterwards, and it makes a nice gift that way too.

Safety information (PDF)

Does it work with oat drink?
Yes. Barista versions of oat or soy drink foam best.
Can I heat milk in it on the hob?
No. The LUN is meant for steaming with a steam wand and shouldn't go on the hob, on induction or in the microwave.
Why is there no blue?
We had a blue sample, but the colour didn't hold up well in our dishwasher tests, so we left it out.

Milk and latte art

Steaming milk, step by step

For latte art you need milk with very fine foam. It's glossy, looks almost like wet paint and has no visible bubbles. This is how we go about it.

  1. Start cold: Take milk straight from the fridge. The colder it is, the more time you have while steaming, and the finer the foam gets. Don't fill the jug too high, the milk needs room to rise.
  2. Add air: Hold the steam wand just below the surface and turn on the steam. Now air is getting into the milk and you hear a quiet, even sound. This only takes a few seconds. If it gets loud and the milk spits, the wand is too high.
  3. Get the milk moving: Then push the wand a little deeper into the milk and tilt the jug slightly. The milk starts to spin. This swirl spreads the foam evenly and breaks up large bubbles. Keep a hand on the jug while you do this. As soon as it's too hot to touch, turn off the steam.
  1. Tap and swirl: Tap the jug once on the worktop to pop any bubbles that are left. Then swirl the milk around a few times until the surface has an even shine.
  2. Pour: Start pouring from a little height into the middle of the cup so the milk and espresso mix. Once the cup is about half full, bring the spout right down to the surface. Now the milk stays on top and the pattern forms. To finish, lift the jug a little and draw through the pattern once.

If it doesn't work yet

  • The foam has big bubbles: Usually too much air got in, or the wand stayed at the surface too long. Next time, push it deeper a little earlier so the milk starts to spin.
  • The milk sinks in the cup and no pattern appears: The spout is still too far from the surface while you pour. Bring the jug closer to the cup.
  • The pattern spreads out: The milk was too thin. Let in a little more air next time.

Not every pattern works at the start, that's the same for everyone. With a bit of practice it gets better quickly.

What fits in the LUN

  • Flat white: double espresso with a little very fine milk. One fill is enough for two.
  • Cappuccino: a bit more foam than a flat white. One fill is enough for one.
  • Matcha latte: whisk matcha smooth with a little hot water and pour the steamed milk in. Works the same way with cocoa.
